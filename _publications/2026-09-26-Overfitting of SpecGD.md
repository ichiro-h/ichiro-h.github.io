We analyze SpecGD in overparameterized matrix classification with corrupted labels under collapsed and dispersed shortcut geometries. In the dispersed model, vanishing shortcut correlations generate a statistically significant signal through a second-order effect, whereas exact orthogonality eliminates the signal entirely; in the collapsed model, the limiting geometry is qualitatively different and can favor SpecGD over GD. To identify these limiting directions beyond static spectral max-margin theory, we analyze the exponentiated-gradient dynamics of the dual weights and establish directional convergence and generalization guarantees.
